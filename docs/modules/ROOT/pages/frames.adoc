The `com.irurueta.navigation.frames` package (and its `frames.converters` sub-package) models the reference
frames used throughout navigation mathematics — Earth-Centered Inertial (ECI), Earth-Centered Earth-Fixed (ECEF),
North-East-Down (NED), and body frames — plus the converters that translate a position/velocity/attitude
estimate from one frame to another. Every equation on this page, and the code that implements it, follows
xref:reference.adoc#bib-groves2013[Groves, 2013] unless stated otherwise; see
xref:reference.adoc#bibliography[the bibliography] for full citations.

== Coordinate frames

[cols="1,3",options="header"]
|===
|Frame |Definition

|ECI (`ECIFrame`)
|Origin at the Earth's center of mass; axes fixed with respect to the stars (nonrotating). Cartesian position,
velocity, and attitude are resolved in these axes. Inertial (Newtonian) navigation equations only hold in a frame
like this one.

|ECEF (`ECEFFrame`)
|Origin at the Earth's center of mass; axes rotate with the Earth (z-axis along the polar axis, x-axis through the
prime meridian at the equator). Used for satellite/GNSS positions and as the pivot frame between ECI and NED.

|NED / local navigation (`NEDFrame`)
|A local frame whose origin follows the body, with axes pointing North, East, and Down (i.e., curvilinear
latitude/longitude/height plus a locally-level attitude reference). This is the natural frame for expressing a
vehicle's position on the Earth's surface and its heading/pitch/roll.

|Body (`CoordinateTransformation` with `FrameType.BODY_FRAME`)
|Axes fixed to the vehicle (typically x-forward, y-right, z-down). Attitude is expressed as the coordinate
transformation between the body frame and one of the frames above.
|===

`FrameType` enumerates these four frames plus `FrameType.LOCAL_TANGENT_PLANE_FRAME` (the Cartesian tangent-plane
frame produced by `converters.FrameToLocalTangentPlaneTransformationConverter`, useful when a Euclidean
approximation of a small area of the Earth's surface is sufficient).

`ECEFPosition`/`ECEFVelocity`, `NEDPosition`/`NEDVelocity`, and `ECEFPositionAndVelocity` are plain data holders
for the position/velocity half of a frame, used by the position/velocity-only converters below when attitude
doesn't need to be carried along.

== Attitude: the coordinate transformation matrix

`CoordinateTransformation` holds the 3x3 rotation matrix, stem:[\mathbf{C}^\alpha_\beta], that transforms a vector's resolving
axes from frame stem:[\alpha] to frame stem:[\beta]:

[stem]
++++
\mathbf{x}^\beta = \mathbf{C}^\alpha_\beta \mathbf{x}^\alpha
++++

Because it is orthonormal, its inverse is simply its transpose, stem:[\mathbf{C}^\beta_\alpha = (\mathbf{C}^\alpha_\beta)^T]
(`CoordinateTransformation.inverse()`), and successive rotations compose by matrix multiplication:

[stem]
++++
\mathbf{C}^\alpha_\gamma = \mathbf{C}^\beta_\gamma \mathbf{C}^\alpha_\beta \qquad \text{(2.15)}
++++

`CoordinateTransformation.setEulerAngles(roll, pitch, yaw)` builds the matrix from Euler angles stem:[(\phi,
\theta, \psi)] using (2.22):

[stem]
++++
\mathbf{C}^\beta_\alpha =
\begin{pmatrix}
\cos\theta\cos\psi & \cos\theta\sin\psi & -\sin\theta \\
-\cos\phi\sin\psi + \sin\phi\sin\theta\cos\psi & \cos\phi\cos\psi + \sin\phi\sin\theta\sin\psi & \sin\phi\cos\theta \\
\sin\phi\sin\psi + \cos\phi\sin\theta\cos\psi & -\sin\phi\cos\psi + \cos\phi\sin\theta\sin\psi & \cos\phi\cos\theta
\end{pmatrix}
++++

[source,java]
----
var c = new CoordinateTransformation(
        Math.toRadians(2.0),   // roll
        Math.toRadians(-1.0),  // pitch
        Math.toRadians(45.0),  // yaw
        FrameType.BODY_FRAME, FrameType.LOCAL_NAVIGATION_FRAME);

var roll = c.getRollEulerAngle();
var pitch = c.getPitchEulerAngle();
var yaw = c.getYawEulerAngle();

var inverse = c.inverseAndReturnNew(); // NED -> body
----

_API reference:_ `CoordinateTransformation` (link:{javadoc-base}/frames/CoordinateTransformation.html[javadoc],
link:{github-base}/frames/CoordinateTransformation.java[source]) +
`FrameType` (link:{javadoc-base}/frames/FrameType.html[javadoc], link:{github-base}/frames/FrameType.java[source])

`CoordinateTransformation.ecefToNedMatrix(latitude, longitude)` builds the ECEF-to-NED rotation directly from
geodetic latitude stem:[L_b] and longitude stem:[\lambda_b] using (2.150):

[stem]
++++
\mathbf{C}^e_n =
\begin{pmatrix}
-\sin L_b \cos\lambda_b & -\sin L_b \sin\lambda_b & \cos L_b \\
-\sin\lambda_b & \cos\lambda_b & 0 \\
-\cos L_b \cos\lambda_b & -\cos L_b \sin\lambda_b & -\sin L_b
\end{pmatrix}
++++

== Frame converters

Each converter in `frames.converters` implements one leg of the ECI/ECEF/NED position-velocity-attitude
conversion, mirroring a MATLAB script from xref:reference.adoc#bib-groves-matlab[Groves' companion MATLAB code].
`FrameConverter<S, D>` is the common
interface; a `*PositionVelocityConverter` variant of each exists for when only position and velocity (no
attitude) need to be converted.

[cols="2,2,3",options="header"]
|===
|Class |Converts |Key equations

|`ECEFtoNEDFrameConverter` / `ECEFtoNEDPositionVelocityConverter`
|ECEF (Cartesian) stem:[\rightarrow] NED (curvilinear)
|Borkowski closed-form position solution (see below), (2.73) velocity, (2.15) attitude

|`NEDtoECEFFrameConverter` / `NEDtoECEFPositionVelocityConverter`
|NED (curvilinear) stem:[\rightarrow] ECEF (Cartesian)
|(2.105) meridian radius of curvature, (2.112) position, (2.73) velocity, (2.15) attitude

|`ECEFtoECIFrameConverter`
|ECEF stem:[\rightarrow] ECI, accounting for Earth rotation over a time interval
|(2.145) rotation matrix, (2.146) position

|`ECItoECEFFrameConverter`
|ECI stem:[\rightarrow] ECEF, accounting for Earth rotation over a time interval
|(2.145) rotation matrix, (2.146) position, (2.15) attitude

|`FrameToLocalTangentPlaneTransformationConverter`
|ECEF frame stem:[\rightarrow] `EuclideanTransformation3D` about a NED-based local tangent plane
|Built from `CoordinateTransformation` (no new equations)
|===

=== ECEF to NED: curvilinear position from Cartesian position

Converting a Cartesian ECEF position stem:[(x^e_{eb}, y^e_{eb}, z^e_{eb})] to curvilinear latitude/longitude/height
is, in general, iterative — (2.113) in xref:reference.adoc#bib-groves2013[Groves, 2013] gives the standard
iterative inverse, noting that the transverse radius of curvature stem:[R_E] itself depends on the (unknown)
latitude:

[stem]
++++
\tan L_b = \frac{z^e_{eb} / \left[R_E(L_b) + h_b\right]}{\sqrt{(x^e_{eb})^2 + (y^e_{eb})^2} \big/ \left[(1-e^2)R_E(L_b) + h_b\right]}
\qquad \tan\lambda_b = \frac{y^e_{eb}}{x^e_{eb}}
\qquad \text{(2.113)}
++++

`ECEFtoNEDFrameConverter`/`ECEFtoNEDPositionVelocityConverter` avoid the iteration by implementing
**Borkowski's closed-form solution** (xref:reference.adoc#bib-borkowski1989[Borkowski, 1989]), referenced by
xref:reference.adoc#bib-groves2013[Groves, 2013] as Appendix C, Section C.2.1, equations `(C.29)-(C.38)` — an
appendix distributed only on the book's companion DVD, not in the print/PDF edition. The chain of intermediate
terms, following the code's own derivation in
https://github.com/albertoirurueta/irurueta-navigation/blob/master/src/main/java/com/irurueta/navigation/frames/converters/ECEFtoNEDFrameConverter.java[`ECEFtoNEDFrameConverter.java`],
is:

[stem]
++++
\begin{aligned}
k_1 &= \sqrt{1-e^2}\,|z^e_{eb}| \qquad k_2 = e^2 R_0 \qquad \beta = \sqrt{(x^e_{eb})^2 + (y^e_{eb})^2} \\
e_c &= \frac{k_1 - k_2}{\beta} \qquad f_c = \frac{k_1 + k_2}{\beta} \qquad p = \frac{4}{3}(e_c f_c + 1) \\
q &= 2(e_c^2 - f_c^2) \qquad d = p^3 + q^2 \qquad v = \sqrt[3]{\sqrt{d} - q} - \sqrt[3]{\sqrt{d} + q} \\
g &= \tfrac{1}{2}\left(\sqrt{e_c^2 + v} + e_c\right) \qquad t = \sqrt{g^2 + \frac{f_c - v g}{2g - e_c}} - g \\
L_b &= \operatorname{sign}(z^e_{eb}) \arctan\!\left(\frac{1-t^2}{2t\sqrt{1-e^2}}\right)
\end{aligned}
++++

with height recovered from stem:[t] and stem:[\beta]. Longitude is simply
stem:[\lambda_b = \operatorname{atan2}(y^e_{eb}, x^e_{eb})] — the same relation as in (2.113). Velocity is then
rotated with (2.73), stem:[\mathbf{v}^n_{eb} = \mathbf{C}^e_n \mathbf{v}^e_{eb}], and attitude with (2.15), stem:[\mathbf{C}^b_n = \mathbf{C}^e_n \mathbf{C}^b_e].

[source,java]
----
var ecef = new ECEFFrame(x, y, z, vx, vy, vz);
var ned = ECEFtoNEDFrameConverter.convertECEFtoNEDAndReturnNew(ecef);

var latitude = ned.getLatitude();
var longitude = ned.getLongitude();
var height = ned.getHeight();
----

=== NED to ECEF: Cartesian position from curvilinear position

The reverse direction is direct, not iterative. The meridian radius of curvature (used for the north-south rate
of change of latitude) is (2.105):

[stem]
++++
R_N(L_b) = \frac{R_0(1-e^2)}{\left(1 - e^2\sin^2 L_b\right)^{3/2}}
++++

and the transverse radius of curvature stem:[R_E(L_b) = R_0 / \sqrt{1 - e^2\sin^2 L_b}] feeds directly into the
Cartesian position (2.112):

[stem]
++++
\begin{aligned}
x^e_{eb} &= \left[R_E(L_b) + h_b\right]\cos L_b \cos\lambda_b \\
y^e_{eb} &= \left[R_E(L_b) + h_b\right]\cos L_b \sin\lambda_b \\
z^e_{eb} &= \left[(1-e^2)R_E(L_b) + h_b\right]\sin L_b
\end{aligned}
\qquad \text{(2.112)}
++++

[source,java]
----
var ned = new NEDFrame(Math.toRadians(41.3851), Math.toRadians(2.1734), 50.0);
var ecef = NEDtoECEFFrameConverter.convertNEDtoECEFAndReturnNew(ned);

var x = ecef.getX();
var y = ecef.getY();
var z = ecef.getZ();
----

=== ECEF and ECI: accounting for Earth rotation

ECI and ECEF share the same origin and z-axis, differing only by the Earth's rotation angle
stem:[\omega_{ie}(t - t_0)] accumulated over the propagation interval.
`CoordinateTransformation.ecefToEciMatrixFromAngle(angle)` implements (2.145):

[stem]
++++
\mathbf{C}^i_e =
\begin{pmatrix}
\cos\omega_{ie}\Delta t & \sin\omega_{ie}\Delta t & 0 \\
-\sin\omega_{ie}\Delta t & \cos\omega_{ie}\Delta t & 0 \\
0 & 0 & 1
\end{pmatrix}
++++

and position transforms with (2.146), stem:[\mathbf{r}^e_{eb} = \mathbf{C}^i_e\, \mathbf{r}^i_{ib}] (and its inverse for ECI stem:[\to] ECEF).

[source,java]
----
var eci = new ECIFrame(x, y, z, vx, vy, vz);
var propagationInterval = new Time(1.0, TimeUnit.SECOND);
var ecef = ECItoECEFFrameConverter.convertECItoECEFAndReturnNew(eci, propagationInterval);
----
